Candidate analytic lemma, independently derived after access repair.
This lemma concerns five arbitrary inner radii in the balanced circular order;
it does not assume equal radii or D5 symmetry. Its role in a full proof still requires
rigorous exclusion of every radial box outside its neighborhood and all other orders.

Let h=pi/5, a=csc(h)=sqrt(2+2/sqrt(5)), rho=rho_star, alpha=asin(1/rho),
b(r)=acos((rho*rho+r*r-4)/(2*rho*r)), and
c(r,s)=acos((r*r+s*s-4)/(2*r*s)). Put delta=1/100.
Suppose the five inner radii r_i lie in [a-delta,a+delta], and the circular order
has exactly two boundary centers of radius rho between each pair of successive
inner centers. Let g_i be that successive inner angular gap. The all-pair constraints
give both g_i>=c(r_i,r_{i+1}) and g_i>=b(r_i)+2*alpha+b(r_{i+1}).

At r=s=a both lower bounds equal 2h, by the classical construction geometry.
Write x_i=r_i-a and z_i=x_i+x_{i+1}. At (a,a), p=partial_r c(a,a)<-2/5,
whereas q=b'(a)>2/5. On the full neighborhood, the Hessian of c has operator norm
at most 1 and |b''|<6. Taylor's theorem consequently gives
c(r_i,r_{i+1}) >= 2h+p*z_i - (x_i*x_i+x_{i+1}*x_{i+1})/2,
b(r_i)+2alpha+b(r_{i+1}) >= 2h+q*z_i -3*(x_i*x_i+x_{i+1}*x_{i+1}).
Taking the larger lower bound yields
g_i >= 2h+(2/5)*|z_i|-3*(x_i*x_i+x_{i+1}*x_{i+1}).

The odd cycle is decisive: 2*x_i = z_i-z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}-z_{i+3}+z_{i+4}.
Thus sum|x_i| <= (5/2)*sum|z_i|, and since |x_i|<=delta,
sum x_i*x_i <= (5*delta/2)*sum|z_i|. Summing the five gap bounds gives
2*pi = sum g_i >= 2*pi + (2/5-15*delta)*sum|z_i|
                     = 2*pi + (1/4)*sum|z_i|.
Therefore every z_i=0 and, because the cycle is odd, every x_i=0.
The inner radii are all exactly a. The gap constraints are then all equalities,
forcing the classical angular gaps and pair tangencies. In particular this
neighborhood admits no configuration whose ALL pairwise distances exceed 2.

Explicit derivative bounds (to be checked algebraically in the eventual proof):
The neighborhood lies inside r,s in [1.69,1.72], rho in [3.52,3.53].
For C=(r*r+s*s-4)/(2*r*s), 0<C<.34, sin(c)>.94,
|C_r|<.43, |C_rr|<.15, |C_rs|<.60. Hence |c_rr|<.25 and |c_rs|<.75,
so the symmetric Hessian has operator norm at most 1.
For B=(rho*rho+r*r-4)/(2*rho*r), 0<B<.96, sin(b)>.28,
|B_r|<.28 and |B_rr|<.5. Thus |b''|<.5/.28+.96*.28*.28/(.28^3)<6.
At a, p=-2/(a^3*sin(2h)); since a<1.705 and a^3<5, p<-2/5.
Also B(a)>.9 gives sin(b(a))<.5, and -B_r(a)>.25 gives q>.5>2/5.
All decimal endpoints above are terminating rationals, so these claims admit
direct rational checks. The lemma is not yet being labeled a breakthrough or outcome.
