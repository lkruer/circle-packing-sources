A0053: failed scalar-shadow inequality, preserved to avoid reusing a false claim

At fixed rho, a point of radius r forbids boundary-center angles within
b(r)=acos((rho^2+r^2-4)/(2rho*r)) of its ray, when the arccos argument is <1.
With alpha=asin(1/rho), a tempting necessary angular excess potential is
sum max(2b(r_i)-2alpha,0) for five inner centers separated by at least 2.
The conjectured lower bound 2pi-20alpha is FALSE. A numerical feasible inner
configuration (shadow-probe-5.json) has four radii essentially
E=sqrt(rho^2-1)-sqrt(3)=1.6443311129 and one radius about 1.9248729.
Its potential is 0.35800285, less than the classical pentagon's 0.52430612.
The experiment is a counterexample to this proposed relaxed inequality,
not a packing of all fifteen disks. The phase of the outer angular positions
is indispensable; scalar total shadow length loses that information.

A different relaxation for five inner centers with exactly two boundary
centers between successive inner rays has edge cost
max{acos((r^2+s^2-4)/(2rs)), b(r)+b(s)+2alpha}.
Numerical minimization on r_i in [E,D] repeatedly finds the classical
pentagon as the minimum, summing to 2pi. On the larger range [rho-2,D],
the same relaxation fails: three radii rho-2 and two about 2.14158317 give
sum 5.8721576754<2pi. The invalidating issue is that shadows shorter than
alpha do not enforce separation across neighboring allowed arcs. None of
these experiments proves an inequality for all actual packings.
